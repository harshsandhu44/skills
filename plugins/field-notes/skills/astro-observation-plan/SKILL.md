# Astro Observation Plan

A good night under the sky is mostly made beforehand: knowing *what's up*, *when it's
highest*, and *whether the conditions will cooperate*. This skill turns a date and a
location into a practical session plan.

Ask for the **date, location, and gear** if not given — targets and timing depend
entirely on them. Reason from known astronomy (what's seasonally visible, Moon phase
effects); flag anything that needs a live check (tonight's exact weather, current comet
positions) rather than asserting it.

## Process

### 1. Establish the conditions

- **Location & light pollution** — rough Bortle level sets what's realistic: a city
  balcony does the Moon and planets; a dark site opens up galaxies and the Milky Way.
- **Date & Moon phase** — the Moon is the biggest variable. A bright Moon washes out
  faint deep-sky objects (plan the Moon itself, doubles, planets); a new Moon is for
  galaxies and nebulae. Note moonrise/moonset.
- **Weather & seeing** — clear skies are necessary but not sufficient: *transparency*
  (haze) matters for faint objects, *seeing* (atmospheric steadiness) for planetary
  detail. Flag these to check on the night.

### 2. Pick the targets

Choose targets suited to the conditions and gear, and prioritize:

- **Solar system** — Moon (terminator detail), visible planets (and events: Jupiter's
  moons, Saturn's rings, a conjunction), the ISS or notable satellite passes.
- **Deep sky** — what's well-placed this season: bright clusters, nebulae, galaxies,
  scaled to the sky darkness and aperture.
- **Events** — meteor showers near peak, a comet, an occultation or eclipse if timed.

For each, note why it's worth it tonight and the minimum gear to enjoy it.

### 3. Time the session

- **When each target is highest** (best seeing, least atmosphere) — observe objects near
  the meridian, avoid low-altitude haze.
- A rough **running order** through the night (e.g. planets at dusk, deep-sky after full
  dark and after moonset), accounting for what sets early.

### 4. Gear & prep

Match kit to plan: binoculars vs telescope (and aperture), eyepieces/filters (e.g. a
lunar or nebula filter), a red light to keep dark adaptation, star chart/app, and the
comfort/safety basics (warm layers, seat, power, someone knows where you are). For
astrophotography, add mount/tracking, exposure approach, and target framing.

## Output shape

```text
## Conditions      (location/Bortle, Moon phase + rise/set, weather/seeing to check)
## Targets         (prioritized; each: why tonight + min gear)
## Timing          (when each is best; running order through the night)
## Gear & prep     (kit list, filters, dark-adaptation, comfort/safety)
## Check on the night  (live items: weather, transparency, any time-critical event)
```

Prioritize ruthlessly by what the conditions actually allow — three targets you'll
genuinely see well beat a list of twenty the Moon will drown out.
